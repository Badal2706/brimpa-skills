# Upadacitinib for severe alopecia areata

## The point in one line
An oral JAK inhibitor already used for rheumatoid arthritis regrew at least 80% scalp hair in about half of patients with severe alopecia areata in two phase 3 trials, and is approved in the EU (July 2026); this is for autoimmune patchy/total hair loss, not ordinary male-pattern baldness.

## Facts (verified)
- Trials UP-AA1 and UP-AA2, published online in JAMA Dermatology on 12 Aug 2026; 1,399 patients aged 12-64 with severe alopecia areata; 24 weeks.
- Reaching SALT20 (80% or more of the scalp covered) at week 24: about 45% on 15 mg and 54-55% on 30 mg, versus 1.5-3.4% on placebo.
- Eyebrow and eyelash regrowth also improved, mainly on 30 mg (per the source post).
- European Commission approval for adults and adolescents 12+ with severe alopecia areata announced 29 Jul 2026 (CHMP positive opinion 29 Jun 2026), at 15 mg and 30 mg once daily. US FDA decision was still pending at that time.

## What the social post got wrong or blurred
- "Bald people can grow their hair back" overstates it: the trials are in **alopecia areata** (an autoimmune disease), not androgenetic (pattern) baldness.
- "At least 80% hair cover for patients" is the share of patients reaching SALT20, not everyone.

## Caveats
- Prescription only; JAK inhibitors carry warnings for serious infections, blood clots, heart events and some cancers in certain groups. The 30 mg arm had more adverse events.
- Indian availability and price for this indication were not checked.
- Not medical advice; a dermatologist decides.
