# Everyday care: hair, teeth, seeds

## The point in one line
Mostly reasonable habits, with two fixes: oiling does not treat dandruff (it can feed the yeast behind it), and Indian fluoride toothpaste is capped at 1000 ppm, so any fluoride paste does the job; the expensive kit is optional.

## Seasonal hair fall (reel's whiteboard: season -> scalp -> cause -> what to do)
| Season | Reel says | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Monsoon | Oily scalp -> fungus. Shampoo twice a week, oil twice a month | Direction right: humidity and oil favour Malassezia yeast. Washing 2-3 times a week or more is fine; use an anti-dandruff shampoo (ketoconazole 2%, zinc pyrithione, selenium sulfide) if it itches or flakes |
| Summer | Heat-sensitive, follicle "contracts". Wash daily and cover hair outdoors | Daily wash with a mild shampoo and sun/dust cover are fine. "Follicle contracts" has no support |
| Winter | Dry -> dandruff. Oil once a week, shampoo once a week | **Weak.** Dry flaking and true dandruff differ. Dandruff (seborrhoeic) is driven by Malassezia, which feeds on scalp oil, so oiling can worsen it and once-a-week washing is too little; medicated shampoo 2-3 times a week is the evidence-based fix. Oil only the lengths if the hair itself is dry |
| Season change | Scalp adapting -> temporary hair fall. Be patient | Supported: studies of healthy people find more hairs in the resting (telogen) phase in summer and more shedding in late summer/autumn. Normal shedding is roughly 50-100 hairs a day |
See a dermatologist if shedding lasts more than about 3 months, comes in patches, or the scalp is red or scaly. **Alopecia areata is not seasonal hair fall**: these routines do not treat it (see health/upadacitinib-alopecia-areata.md).

## Budget oral-care kit (dentist's list, prices as stated, Aug 2026)
| Item | Reel's pick and price | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Toothpaste | Colgate Total, Oral-B and similar (about Rs 200), or any simple 1000 ppm fluoride paste for everyday use | Fluoride is what matters. BIS (IS 6356) caps fluoride in Indian toothpaste at 1000 ppm, so any fluoride paste meets it; international guidance for adults is 1350-1500 ppm where allowed. Spit, do not rinse, after brushing |
| Water flosser | Agaro, about Rs 1,600, "cheapest, two people can use it" | Studies show water flossers reduce gum bleeding at least as well as string floss; good for braces, bridges and implants. Sharing works only with separate tips |
| Electric toothbrush | Oral-B rechargeable (price unclear in audio, heard as about Rs 1,600); fancier brands optional | Cochrane review (2014): oscillating-rotating powered brushes remove about 21% more plaque and reduce gingivitis about 11% more than manual at 3 months. Nice to have, not required |
| Manual toothbrush | GuruNanda "butter on gums" (about Rs 120, says ADA-accepted on the pack), otherwise Colgate or Sensodyne soft bristles (about Rs 120) | Soft bristles, small head, replace every 3 months. "ADA accepted" is the brand's claim |
| Tongue cleaner | Copper, about Rs 300 | Any scraper (steel, plastic, copper) works; copper's antimicrobial claim does not matter here |
Total for a good routine: about Rs 2,000-2,500 once plus toothpaste and brush heads; a fluoride paste and soft brush alone (about Rs 300) cover most of the benefit.

## Seeds: how to eat them
| Seed | Reel says | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Flax | Grind, eat with curd | **Correct and important**: whole flaxseed often passes undigested; grind fresh (or store ground flax in the fridge) |
| Chia | Soak, eat with oats; avoids bloating | Soaking is right (dry chia swells and has caused oesophageal blockage in a case report); "no bloating" is anecdotal |
| Pumpkin | Roast, eat with fruit for zinc and magnesium | Roasting is fine; fruit's vitamin C mainly helps iron, not zinc or magnesium. Phytates limit zinc absorption either way |
| Sesame | Crush, eat with amla for calcium and iron | Crushing helps (whole sesame passes through). Amla's vitamin C does improve plant-iron absorption; it does nothing for calcium, and sesame calcium is partly bound by oxalate in the hull |
| Sabja (sweet basil) | Soak, add to shikanji, "cools the body" | Must be soaked. "Cooling" is traditional, not shown in studies beyond the drink's hydration |
