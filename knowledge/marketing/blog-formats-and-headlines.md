# Blog formats, tones, hooks and headlines

## The point in one line
Pick the format from the search intent, open answer-first, and make every headline promise something specific; the lists below are a menu, not a rule book.

## Formats (13)
How-to guide; listicle; "what is" explainer; comparison (X vs Y); review; best-of roundup; case study; checklist; template; opinion; interview; news update; original research. Match to intent: how-to and explainer for informational, comparison/review/best-of for commercial, case study and original research for trust and links, checklist and template for saves and backlinks.

## Tones (8)
Conversational, expert, results-driven, optimistic, witty, casual/Gen-Z, transparent, formal. Pick one per site and keep it; for clinics and B2B, expert + transparent reads best. Run the de-slop pass in skills/launch-marketing regardless of tone.

## Three core elements
1. First-hand experience (E-E-A-T): something only you could know (your data, your photos, your mistakes).
2. Skimmable, answer-first format: the answer in the first lines, then detail.
3. Images, screenshots and video of the real thing.

## Intro types (4)
Answer-first (guides, FAQs); plot twist (opinion, strategy); high-stakes story (case studies, reviews, tutorials); proprietary data (B2B, research).

## Headline types (6), with the reel's examples reworded
| Type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Contrarian | "Why you should stop X" | Why you should stop writing 2,000-word posts |
| Case study | "How we got [result] in [time]" | How a Palanpur clinic filled Tuesday slots in 6 weeks |
| Hyper-specific resource | "The exact [thing] I use for [job]" | The exact outline I use for every service page |
| Friction-killer | "How to [goal] with zero [obstacle]" | How to start a clinic blog with zero budget |
| Data drop | "We analysed [N] [things]. Here's what won" | We checked 40 dentist websites in Gujarat; here is what ranked |
| Expectation vs reality | "[Doing X] for a year: what I expected vs what happened" | A year of Google Business Profile posts: expected vs actual calls |
Rule: the headline is a promise the first screen must keep; never a number you cannot show.

## Content pillars (6)
Blueprint (the complete guide to X); counter-trend (why [common practice] is failing); case study; toolbox (every free tool I use for X); failure analysis (the mistake that cost me Y); future forecast (how [change] affects X). A small business needs two or three pillars, not six.

## Attention triggers (6)
Strong first line; scannable subheadings; short paragraphs; examples or screenshots; real numbers; a clear next step. These are the same as our readability and CTA rules; the point is to check each one before publishing.

## The five prompt templates
The second reel's cards are prompts for writing a how-to guide, a list article, a case study, a comparison post and a problem-solution post, each with a "my details" block, an outline-then-approve step, writing rules and a pre-finish check. They are procedures, so they live in skills/launch-marketing/references/article-templates.md (rewritten with our own rules: no invented prices or rankings, dates next to prices, disclosure of relationships, product mentions only with proof, client named only with permission).

## Caveats
- Both reels end with a "comment for the full guide" hook; the full guides were not obtained and are not needed.
- Formats and headline types are conventions from one creator; they match common content-marketing practice but carry no data.
