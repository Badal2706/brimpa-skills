# Sales calls and startup rules from three reels

## The point in one line
On a sales call, stop pitching after you hear the problem: ask what fixing it in 30 days would change, then what stops them doing it now. The client sells themselves and names their own objections.

## The no-pitch close
1. Ask about the problem and listen (for a clinic: "How do patients book today? What goes wrong?").
2. **"What would happen if you fixed this in the next 30 days?"** They describe the upside in their own words (fewer missed appointments, front desk not on the phone all day, more Google reviews).
3. **"What's stopping you from doing it now?"** They list the objections (budget, time, "my staff won't use it", a cousin who builds websites) before you raise them, so you know exactly what to answer.
4. Offer the path: one proposal that removes those specific obstacles (phased price, you train the staff, you migrate the old data).
Why it works: it matches Neil Rackham's SPIN research (about 35,000 sales calls, published 1988), which found that in larger sales, implication and need-payoff questions predicted success better than describing features. People believe what they say more than what they are told.

## "Make $1M in 90 days with AI" (interview clip)
The advice: do AI consulting for an **underserved industry**, get in front of many owners in it, automate their operations, then look for the repeated job you can turn into a software product ("a business in a box"). This is exactly the Brimpa route: client work for clinics and hospitals (consulting), then the repeatable parts become the hospital-management product. The "$1M in 90 days" and the "$1.4B CEO" title are the clip's framing, not verified figures.

## 13 startup rules (on-screen list)
Build fast. Rejection is data. Marketing is differentiation. Speed kills competition. Distribution beats product. Use Twitter (X) and LinkedIn. Iterate fast. Kill ideas fast. Free users lie (only payment proves demand). Watch every session recording (heard as "postdoc", almost certainly PostHog session replay). Forget colour schemes (early on). Spam UGC (post user-generated-style short videos at volume).
Caveats for Brimpa: "spam UGC" and "forget colour schemes" suit consumer apps; for clinics and hospitals, trust and a clean look sell, and health content has advertising rules (no cure claims, no patient testimonials that break the medical council's rules). Session recordings must mask health and personal fields under DPDP.

## Creative presentation as the differentiator (sales post, Nov 2026)
When several sellers offer similar products, price and quality stop being the only levers; how the offer is presented decides who gets attention. A creative demo, an unexpected approach, different packaging or a playful way of showing the product makes the buyer stop and look at what they would otherwise skip. The product often stays the same; the experience around it changes. A good salesperson watches how people react, notices what sparks interest and adapts without losing trust. For Brimpa: the live demo of Brimpa Care on the clinic's own patient flow, a mock prescription printed with the clinic's name, or a one-page "your site in 14 days" sheet is the presentation; the software is the same.

## Seven broad problem areas worth a business ("15 problems worth billions", part 2 only)
Energy efficiency and clean energy access; environment and pollution; faster, safer, cheaper transport; mental health and well-being; access to food and essentials; turning complicated processes into simple experiences; communities around shared interests. Too broad to act on alone; the usable step is the ABDM note (business/clinic-abdm-onboarding-opportunity.md): find the complicated process one specific group faces every week and remove it. Part 1 (problems 1-8) was not captured.

## Caveats
- The no-pitch close needs real discovery first; asking question 2 before you understand the problem sounds scripted.
- Hormozi's rules are opinions from a US gym-and-software operator; they are recorded as a checklist to argue with, not findings.
- Reels give no evidence for the startup rules; treat them as a checklist of habits, not findings.
