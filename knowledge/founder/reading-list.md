# Founder reading list

## The point in one line
Five books saved from reels, each mapped to a concrete founder skill: talking to clients, not needing approval, selling, endurance, and inner steadiness.

## Details
| # | Book | Author (first published) | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | How to Talk to Anyone | Leil Lowndes (2003) | Small talk and first impressions with clinic owners and doctors at meetings |
| 2 | The Courage to Be Disliked | Ichiro Kishimi & Fumitake Koga (Japanese 2013, English 2018) | Adlerian psychology: separating your tasks from others' judgments; saying no to bad-fit clients |
| 3 | The Greatest Salesman in the World | Og Mandino (1968) | A parable of ten "scrolls" on habits and persistence in selling |
| 4 | Can't Hurt Me | David Goggins (2018) | Discipline and endurance; the "40% rule" and the accountability mirror |
| 5 | Autobiography of a Yogi | Paramahansa Yogananda (1946) | Spiritual memoir; meditation and inner calm (a book Steve Jobs reportedly reread yearly) |

## Example
Before a sales call with a hospital administrator, re-read the Lowndes chapters on first-minute impressions; after losing a deal, the Kishimi-Koga idea of "separation of tasks" helps decide what was in your control.
