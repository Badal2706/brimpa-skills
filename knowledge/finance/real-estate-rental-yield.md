# Rental yield: is the asking price worth it?

## The point in one line
Gross rental yield = annual rent / purchase price x 100. It is the fastest sanity check on whether a property is priced for living in or for speculation.

## Formula
- Monthly rent = R
- Annual rent = R x 12
- Purchase price = P
- Gross yield (%) = (R x 12) / P x 100

## Example (from the source)
- Monthly rent Rs 10,000, so annual rent Rs 1,20,000
- Price Rs 35,00,000
- Yield = 1,20,000 / 35,00,000 x 100 = 3.43%

## How to read the number (general guidance, not from the source)
- Indian residential property commonly yields 2-4% gross; commercial 6-9%. A residential yield under 2.5% means the price is mostly a bet on appreciation.
- Compare with the risk-free alternative (bank FD or government bond rate at the time). If yield plus realistic appreciation does not beat it after costs, the price is high.
- Net yield subtracts maintenance, property tax, society charges, vacancy (assume 1 month a year) and brokerage. Net is often 25-40% lower than gross.
- Price-to-rent ratio is the inverse: P / annual rent. Above 25-30 is expensive in most markets.

## Caveats
- Yield ignores leverage, tax treatment, and appreciation; use it as a filter, not a decision.
- Advertised rents are higher than achieved rents; use actual comparable leases.
