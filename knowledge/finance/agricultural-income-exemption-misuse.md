# Agricultural income exemption: legal use vs laundering

## The point in one line
Genuine agricultural income from land in India is tax-free with no ceiling, but "showing" farm sales in cash to bring unexplained money into the bank is tax evasion and money laundering; the exemption only protects income that really came from farming and can be proved.

## Details

### What the law actually gives (legal, use freely)
| Item | 1961 Act (old number) | Income-tax Act 2025 (in force 1 Apr 2026) | Status of the mapping |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agricultural income exempt (rent/revenue from land in India used for agriculture, income from cultivation, basic processing to make produce marketable, farm buildings, nursery) | s.10(1), definition s.2(1A) | Schedule II, entry 1 (exempt incomes moved to Schedules) | Secondary sources; confirm in the Act text |
| Partial integration: agri income above Rs 5,000 plus non-agri income above the basic exemption limit -> agri income is added only to push the rest into a higher slab | Finance Act rate rules | Same mechanism | Substance verified on incometaxindia.gov.in; numbering not |
| Rural agricultural land is not a "capital asset", so its sale is outside capital-gains tax; urban agricultural land (within 2/6/8 km of a municipality depending on population) is taxable | s.2(14) | Same concept | Substance well established |
| Rollover relief when urban agricultural land is sold and proceeds reinvested in agricultural land (land used for farming for 2 years before sale; new land held 3 years) | s.54B | s.83 | Two secondary sources; conditions not re-checked for the 2025 text |
| Cash receipt limit: no person may receive Rs 2 lakh or more in cash from one person in a day, for one transaction, or for one event; penalty = 100% of the amount, on the receiver | s.269ST, penalty s.271DA | s.186, penalty s.451 | Several secondary tables agree; confirm before quoting |

Note: dairy, poultry and cattle-rearing income is generally NOT agricultural income (it is business income) unless it is incidental to cultivation; the reel's "keep cows and sell milk as agricultural income" is wrong on its face.

### What the reel described (presented as satire; every step below is illegal when the income is not real)
1. Buy agricultural land (claiming capital-gains relief).
2. "Show" agricultural sales in cash, e.g. one litre of milk "sold" for Rs 1.9 lakh to an unnamed buyer, keeping each receipt under Rs 2 lakh so no PAN is quoted.
3. Deposit the cash as tax-free "farm income"; scale up via a gaushala (cow shelter) run as a charity, claiming donation deductions and depositing cash from "milk" and "dung" sales.
4. Accept small fines to "prove farmer status".
The creator's own captions said "I am not promoting money laundering" and "please save this country and its farmers' rights".

### Why it fails (and what it costs)
- Assessing officers ask for land records (7/12 extract, khasra/khatauni), crop pattern, yield per acre for that district, mandi/APMC receipts, buyer identities, input purchases and bank trail. The CAG's 2019 audit (6,778 cases sampled, FY to March 2018) found 22.5% of agricultural-income claims had been allowed without adequate verification; Parliament's Public Accounts Committee (April 2022) told the department to verify strictly. Scrutiny of large farm-income claims has tightened since.
- Unexplained cash credits and investments are taxed at a flat punitive rate plus penalty (old ss.68-69 with s.115BBE; renumbered in the 2025 Act), and fake claims can attract prosecution and, for proceeds of crime, the PMLA.
- Yield plausibility is the first test. Benchmark (added 2026-10-30): India's average coconut productivity was **9,249 nuts per hectare per year** in 2024-25 (DA&FW statistics as reported by PIB/press, Aug 2026). A Hindi farming reel (Oct 2026) promised "Rs 2.5 lakh a month" from 3 bigha of coconut (5,000 nuts x Rs 50); 3 bigha is roughly 0.5-0.8 ha depending on the state, so about 5,000-7,000 nuts **a year**, i.e. at most Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh a year at a generous Rs 50 farm-gate price, and only after the palms start bearing (roughly 3-4 years for dwarf types such as the "Malaysian" dwarf the reel recommends, longer for tall varieties; general horticulture figure, not checked for this note). The reel's figure is per year mislabelled as per month. A return claiming farm income far above district yields invites exactly this arithmetic.
- Splitting a large sale into many sub-Rs 2 lakh receipts to dodge the cash limit is itself the evasion pattern the rule targets; the "one event" test catches it.
- Cash donations above Rs 2,000 to a charity do not qualify for the donation deduction (old s.80G); anonymous donations to most trusts are taxed at 30%.
- Buying agricultural land in states such as Maharashtra needs "agriculturist" status under state land laws; false status claims are a revenue-law violation separate from tax.

## Example
A clinic owner earns Rs 30 lakh from practice and owns 5 acres of genuinely cultivated rural land that yields Rs 3 lakh of produce sold through the local mandi with receipts. Legal: report the Rs 3 lakh as exempt agricultural income in the return (partial integration will raise the slab rate on the Rs 30 lakh slightly). Illegal: reporting Rs 40 lakh of "farm income" from the same 5 acres, received in cash from unnamed buyers. The first survives scrutiny; the second is evasion with penalty and possible prosecution.

## Caveats
- Section numbers for the 2025 Act come from secondary mapping tables; read the Act or the CBDT correspondence table before citing them in client work.
- Rules on rural/urban land distance, s.83 conditions and penalties can change by Finance Act. This is not tax advice; involve a CA for real filings.

### Red lines (never advise, never build tools that enable)
- Declaring income that did not come from cultivation as agricultural income.
- Cash receipts structured to stay under Rs 2 lakh or to avoid quoting PAN.
- Fake buyers, fake invoices, fake donation receipts, or charities used to park or route cash.
- False "farmer/agriculturist" declarations to buy land.

### What the source got wrong
- On-screen "Section 54B gives zero capital gains tax when you buy agricultural land": 54B gives relief on the sale of agricultural land when proceeds are reinvested; buying land does not make anything tax-free. From 1 Apr 2026 the sections are renumbered (s.83 per secondary sources).
- On-screen "Section 10(1) IT Act": correct for the 1961 Act only; the 2025 Act moved exempt income into Schedule II.
- Milk and dung sales are generally business income, not agricultural income.
- "Retired chief commissioner Vijay Sharma filed a PIL in Patna High Court in 2009": the only report found (2016) calls him a retired income-tax officer and dates the PIL to about 2016; no outcome was found. His RTI data was reported to show farm-income claims many times India's agricultural output, but the published figures were inconsistent (Rs 2,000 vs 674 vs 199 lakh crore), so treat "claims exceed agricultural GDP" as alleged, not established.
- Claims about named politicians and celebrities (a sting operation, a film family's Alibaug farmland, cricketers and actors) are allegations or reported probes with no findings; they are left out of this note on purpose.
- Context the reel left out: a Delhi High Court PIL asking for curbs on misuse of the exemption was dismissed on 13 May 2026 as "misconceived", because courts cannot direct the legislature to make a law.
