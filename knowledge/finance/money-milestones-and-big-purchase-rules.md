# Money milestones and big-purchase rules of thumb

## The point in one line
The milestone list is a sensible order (cash buffer, then investments, then big purchases, then second income), but the rules attached to it are rules of thumb, the SIP table assumes a generous 13% return, and a lower EMI from a balloon loan is a postponed payment, not a saving.

## The nine milestones (from the reel), with our adjustment
| # | Milestone | Our note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rs 1 lakh in the bank | Fine as a first step. For an irregular-income freelancer the real target is 9-12 months of essentials plus 2-3 months of business costs (finance/freelancer-money-system.md). Get health insurance first. |
| 2 | Rs 10 lakh investment portfolio ("compounding starts showing") | See the SIP table below |
| 3 | First international trip | Pay from a sinking fund, not a card you revolve |
| 4 | First car | Rules below |
| 5 | First house | Rules below; also finance/first-property-purchase-checklist.md and finance/real-estate-rental-yield.md |
| 6 | Second source of income (freelancing, rental, side business) | For Badal this is the Brimpa product line beside client work |
| 7 | Passive income above monthly expenses | i.e. financial independence for running costs |
| 8 | Rs 1 crore in assets (MFs, property, gold...) | Count net worth, not gross assets: subtract loans |
| 9 | Rs 10 crore net worth | |

## Emergency cash: where to keep it
- The reel's bank-rate table (SBI 2.5%; IDFC FIRST up to 6.5%; RBL up to 5.64%; others 2.5-5%) is indicative only. 2026 comparison sites disagree (IDFC FIRST quoted between 6.5% and 7.5%; RBL between 5.5% and 7%), and top rates apply only to slabs of the balance, not the whole amount.
- Deposit insurance (DICGC) covers up to Rs 5 lakh per depositor per bank (limit set Feb 2020; no 2026 increase found); keep a large buffer split across banks or in a sweep FD / liquid fund (as in the money-system note).

## First Rs 10 lakh by SIP (recomputed)
Monthly SIP, raised 7% every year (step-up), years to reach Rs 10 lakh:
| SIP | at 13% a year (reel's assumption) | at 12% | at 10% | at 10%, no step-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rs 5,000 | 7.9 (reel: 8) | 8.1 | 8.5 | 9.9 |
| Rs 10,000 | 5.3 (reel: 5) | 5.3 | 5.5 | 6.2 |
| Rs 15,000 | 3.9 (reel: 4) | 4.0 | 4.2 | 4.5 |
The reel's table is arithmetically right; 13% is an optimistic equity assumption, and over 4-8 years equity returns can be far below average. The contribution amount matters more than the assumed return at this stage.

## Car: rules of thumb
- Reel's rule: **30% down payment, loan tenure within 5 years, car EMI at most 15% of income.**
- The common international rule is **20/4/10** (20% down, at most 4 years, all car costs at most 10% of gross income). Both say the same thing: a car is a depreciating asset; keep the loan short and small.
- Recommendation: use the stricter of the two for a variable-income founder (30% down, at most 4-5 years, EMI at most 10-15% of the *salary you pay yourself*, not of business receipts).

## House: rules of thumb
- Reel's rule: **price at most 5x annual household income; at least 20% down; tenure at most 25 years; all home-loan EMIs at most 40% of income.**
- Regulatory backstop: RBI caps loan-to-value at 90% for loans up to Rs 30 lakh, 80% for Rs 30-75 lakh and 75% above Rs 75 lakh, so 10-25% down is compulsory anyway, plus stamp duty and registration (which banks do not fund). Lenders' FOIR (all EMIs / income) limits are typically 40-50%; 40% is the safe end.
- Buy vs rent: the reel's pros/cons list is balanced (rental yields in Indian cities around 2-3%, stamp duty 4-8%, illiquidity, career mobility vs stability and forced saving). Use finance/real-estate-rental-yield.md for the price test.

## Balloon ("residual value") car loan - the maths
Example from the reel: Rs 60 lakh car, Rs 10 lakh down, Rs 50 lakh loan at 9% for 60 months.
| | Normal loan | Balloon: Rs 24 lakh deferred to month 60 |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly EMI | **Rs 1,03,792** | **about Rs 71,970** (on-screen: Rs 71,826) |
| Lump sum at month 60 | 0 | Rs 24,00,000 |
| Total interest | **about Rs 12.3 lakh** | **about Rs 17.2 lakh** |
| Difference | | EMI about Rs 31,800 lower; about Rs 4.9 lakh more interest |

- Why the EMI drops: you pay interest on the full Rs 50 lakh for all five years, but repay only Rs 26 lakh of principal through EMIs; the Rs 24 lakh (roughly the lender's estimate of the car's value after five years) is due in one go at the end.
- **Break-even:** investing the Rs 31,800 a month for 60 months grows to exactly Rs 24 lakh at 9% a year. So the balloon only pays off if your investments beat **the loan rate after tax**, with risk. At 7% (debt) you end about Rs 1.2 lakh short; at 12% pre-tax equity you end about Rs 2 lakh ahead before tax, with no guarantee.
- In India it is offered mainly on luxury cars through the maker's finance arm with an **assured buyback** (BMW, Mercedes-Benz "Agility", Audi): at the end you keep the car (pay or refinance the balloon), trade in, or return it. Buyback values depend on model, mileage and condition; banks reportedly cap the balloon at about 50% of the loan and 60 months for new cars (secondary sources).
- Risks: if the car is worth less than the balloon (accident, high mileage, market drop) and there is no assured buyback, you owe more than the car is worth; refinancing the balloon at month 60 depends on rates then.
- For a founder: the honest use case is a business that values monthly cash flow over total cost and will trade the car in at the end anyway. For personal finance, a cheaper car beats a clever loan.

## Caveats
- Rules of thumb, not personal advice. Run your own numbers with money-guide.
- Savings rates and card/loan offers change; check the bank's page on the day.
- Stamp duty and registration figures: see finance/first-property-purchase-checklist.md (state-specific).

## What the source got wrong
- Milestones reel: "Rs 1 lakh in the bank" as the emergency milestone is too small for irregular income; and "compounding magic starts only after Rs 10 lakh" is a framing - compounding works from the first rupee; it just becomes visible later.
- Milestones reel: savings-rate table shows top-slab rates as if they applied to the full balance; current rates differ across sources.
- Car-loan reel: the spoken normal EMI was transcribed as "1,38,000", but the on-screen figure **Rs 1,03,800** is correct. The on-screen card says "EMI is calculated only on the remaining amount (Rs 26 lakh)" - wrong: EMI on Rs 26 lakh alone would be about Rs 53,970; the Rs 71,826-71,970 EMI includes interest on the deferred Rs 24 lakh every month.
- Car-loan reel: "the bank never tells you" - makers' finance arms advertise these schemes openly.
- Car-loan reel: the justification "they think they can earn more than Rs 5 lakh from the Rs 31,800" leaves out that the hurdle is the loan rate after tax, with market risk.
