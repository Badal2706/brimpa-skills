# Salary-multiplier money rules

## The point in one line
Useful as a quick sanity check, but the freedom number must be built on **expenses, not salary**, India needs a bigger multiple than the US "4% rule", and the reel's percentages add up to more than 100% unless rent sits inside the 50%.

## The six rules as shown (example: Rs 60,000 a month)
| # | Rule | Example | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monthly salary x 300 = the amount that, invested, means you never need to work again | Rs 1,80,00,000 | x300 monthly = 25x annual salary = the US 4% rule. For India use **30-35x annual expenses** (a 3-3.5% withdrawal rate), because Indian inflation runs about 5-6% against the 2-3% the 4% rule was built on. Using salary instead of spending overstates the number if you save a lot and understates it if your lifestyle grows. |
| 2 | Salary x 0.5 = maximum for living costs (groceries, bills, transport) | Rs 30,000 | Fine as a "needs" cap (the 50/30/20 idea). |
| 3 | Salary x 0.05 = guilt-free fun money | Rs 3,000 | Fine; a fixed fun budget makes the rest of the plan easier to keep. |
| 4 | Monthly **expenses** x 3 = minimum emergency fund in a savings account | Rs 90,000 (the Rs 30,000 expenses from rule 2, x 3) | 3 months is a floor for a salaried person. For irregular income (freelancer, founder) keep 6-12 months; see finance/freelancer-money-system.md. A sweep-in FD or liquid fund is fine for most of it. |
| 5 | Salary x 0.35 = maximum rent | Rs 21,000 | High for India if it is on top of rule 2. Treat rent as part of the 50% "needs", or keep it to about 25-30% of take-home. |
| 6 | Salary x 0.20 = invest every month for long-term wealth | Rs 12,000 | Good minimum. Increase it with each raise (step-up SIP). |

## Example: the reel's numbers do not add up
Rs 30,000 + Rs 3,000 + Rs 21,000 + Rs 12,000 = Rs 66,000, which is more than the Rs 60,000 salary. A version that balances: needs 50% (rent included) Rs 30,000; wants/fun 10-15% Rs 6,000-9,000; investing 20% Rs 12,000; the rest goes to the emergency fund until it is full, then to goals (insurance premiums, a car or home down payment).

## Freedom number done properly
1. Annual spending today (be honest; include insurance, travel, family support).
2. Multiply by 30-35 for India (the US convention is 25).
3. Inflate it to the year you want freedom (at 6% a year, costs double in about 12 years).
4. The withdrawal rate is industry opinion, not settled science: only one formal India study exists (Saraogi, SSRN), and it found historical rates often above 4% but argues for lower rates because of India's short market history and falling real returns. Recheck the plan every few years.

## Caveats
- "Never work again" assumes a diversified portfolio (mostly equity early), no large one-off costs (medical, children's education) and discipline in bad years. Health insurance outside the corpus is essential.
- Tax on withdrawals (LTCG on equity above the exemption, slab tax on debt fund gains) lowers the usable rate. See skills/money-guide.

## What the source got wrong
- Freedom number based on salary, with the US multiple (x300 = 25x annual).
- Rule 4 (3 months of expenses) is presented as enough for everyone; it is the floor for a stable salary, too little for irregular income.
- The parts add up to 110% of salary unless rent is inside the 50%.
