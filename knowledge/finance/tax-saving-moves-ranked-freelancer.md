# Legal tax-saving moves, ranked (Indian IT freelancer, TY 2026-27)

## The point in one line
For a solo IT freelancer, the moves that matter are structural: stay presumptive (s.58), take every rupee by bank, choose the regime with numbers, and pay advance tax on time. Deductions such as 80C rarely matter now, because the new regime usually wins.

## Ranked list

| # | Move | Rupee value (worked) | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stay on s.58 Sl. 3 presumptive (50%) while receipts are at most Rs 75 lakh and cash at most 5% | Tax is 0-9.8% of receipts; at Rs 70 lakh, Rs 4.68 lakh less than books at Rs 50 lakh profit | Low |
| 2 | Bank or UPI for every receipt | Keeps the Rs 75 lakh limit and avoids the audit. Cash of Rs 2 lakh or more from one person carries a 100% penalty (s.186/451) | None |
| 3 | Advance tax on time; reconcile TDS with AIS; file on time for refunds | Avoids 1%/month interest (s.424/425). With 10% TDS, refunds are common (Rs 1.77 lakh at Rs 45 lakh receipts) | None |
| 4 | Compare old and new regimes every year | New regime at Rs 45 lakh receipts: Rs 2,73,000 vs old Rs 4,21,200 | Low. With business income, the old-regime option can be withdrawn only once (s.202(4)) |
| 5 | Harvest Rs 1.25 lakh of equity LTCG every FY (s.198); harvest losses before 31 March | Up to Rs 15,625 a year (Rs 16,250 with cess) | None |
| 6 | Salaried + freelance: employer NPS in the CTC (s.124(1)-(2), 14% of basic, allowed in the new regime) | Rs 24,700 on Rs 10 lakh salary + Rs 8 lakh freelance (basic Rs 5 lakh) | Low; money locked |
| 7 | Pay family who genuinely work, at market rate, by bank | Rs 1,87,200 for a Rs 6 lakh fee at Rs 40 lakh profit **on books**. **Zero under presumptive** | Medium; s.99 clubbing if not genuine |
| 8 | Pvt Ltd (s.200, 25.168%) with salary up to Rs 12.75 lakh (nil tax), employer NPS, profit retained | At Rs 40 lakh profit: Rs 1,25,372 a year before Rs 40k-1.5L compliance cost. If profit is paid as dividend, Rs 4,48,450 *less* cash than a proprietor | Medium |
| 9 | DPIIT startup + s.140: 100% profit deduction for 3 of the first 10 years (company or LLP; not under s.200) | High, if profitable | Medium |
| 10 | Old regime only: s.123 Rs 1.5 lakh + s.124(3) Rs 50,000 + s.126 (higher for senior parents) + s.134 rent | Rs 85,800 on a Rs 2.75 lakh stack at 31.2%. Usually beaten by the new regime | Low |
| 11 | LUT for export clients (zero-rated GST, no IGST paid upfront) | Cash flow | Low |
| 12 | Property: s.82/85/86 reinvestment; pre-23-Jul-2024 land or buildings use the lower of 12.5% or 20% indexed | Case-specific | Low |
| 13 | Gifts to parents in a lower bracket to invest (gifts to relatives are tax-free; income on gifts to a spouse is clubbed) | Medium | Low; verify the clubbing detail |

## Aggressive positions (need a written CA opinion, never the default)
- IT services as a "business" at 6%/8% (s.58 Sl. 1) instead of a "profession" at 50% (Sl. 3). At Rs 45 lakh receipts the gap is Rs 2.73 lakh a year.
- Splitting receipts across family members or entities to multiply presumptive limits. This is legitimate only with genuinely separate work and clients.
- A director salary pitched to fill slabs. It must be reasonable for the services.

## Example
Rs 45 lakh receipts, all clients at 10% TDS:
- Presumptive 50% gives deemed income of Rs 22.5 lakh.
- New-regime tax is Rs 2,73,000.
- TDS of Rs 4,50,000 means a refund of Rs 1,77,000 if the return is filed on time.
- Moves 1-5 apply. Moves 7-9 do not yet.

## Caveats
- The ranking assumes a solo developer who spends most of the profit. A product company that retains profit moves the company and DPIIT options up.
- Values use the new-regime slabs, the s.156 rebate, marginal relief and 4% cess as of Oct 2026.
- Not tax advice. Verify with a CA.
