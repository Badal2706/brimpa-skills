# First property purchase: seven checks and the real cost

## The point in one line
Budget about 8-12% above the agreed price for a resale or ready flat (more for under-construction because of GST), get a property lawyer to check title before you pay anything substantial, fix your credit score before you shortlist, and prefer ready-to-move with an occupancy certificate for a first purchase.

## The seven checks (reel's list, with corrections)
1. **Hire a property lawyer after you like a property and before you sign or pay a token.** The lawyer checks title chain (30 years where possible), encumbrance certificate, approved plan, RERA registration, occupancy certificate (OC) and completion certificate, society NOC, pending dues and litigation. The reel's "Rs 18,000 lawyer saves Rs 70 lakh" is an anecdote; typical title-search fees vary by city; the point stands.
2. **See many properties (the reel says at least ten) before deciding.** The anecdote of saving Rs 13 lakh on the eighth viewing for the same size and area is unverified, but comparing comparables is how you learn the real rate; also check the government ready-reckoner / jantri rate for the area.
3. **Budget the all-in cost, not the price.** See the table below. The reel's "+15%" is a safe planning buffer that includes interiors and moving; the statutory part alone is usually 6-8%.
4. **Check your CIBIL score before you start looking.** Lenders set their own cut-offs (no regulatory minimum): most banks want about 700-750+, below about 650 options shrink to NBFCs at much higher rates. The reel's "below 750, spend six months fixing it first" is conservative but sensible; the score also decides your interest rate.
5. **Decide why you are buying: to live in or to invest.** Location, budget and even the loan structure differ; for investment, run the rental-yield test (finance/real-estate-rental-yield.md).
6. **Ready-to-move vs under-construction are different decisions.** First-time buyers: ready-to-move with OC is safer. Under-construction risks: delay, paying EMI and rent together, builder insolvency; benefits: lower price, payment plan. Check the project on the state RERA portal (registration, completion date, quarterly progress, complaints).
7. **Check the society, not just the flat.** Maintenance records, sinking fund, water supply, lift maintenance history, parking allotment, pending disputes; talk to existing residents directly.
- **Bonus: plan resale before buying.** Floor, facing, parking, vastu and carpet-area efficiency matter to the next buyer even if not to you.

## The real cost (2026, indicative; verify on the state portal)
| Item | Typical | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Stamp duty | Gujarat ~4.9%; Maharashtra 5-6% (women ~1% lower); Delhi 6% men, 4% women, 5% joint; Karnataka 2-5% by value slab | Charged on the higher of agreement value and the government guideline (ready-reckoner/jantri) value |
| Registration fee | ~1% (Maharashtra capped at Rs 30,000; Gujarat ~1%) | |
| GST | **Under-construction**: 5% without input credit (1% for affordable housing, carpet area up to 60 sq m metro / 90 sq m non-metro and value up to Rs 45 lakh). **Ready-to-move with OC or resale: no GST** | Biggest hidden difference between the two choices |
| Brokerage | ~1-2% + 18% GST on the brokerage | Negotiable |
| Legal / title check, loan processing | lawyer fee + bank processing ~0.25-1% of loan | |
| Society transfer charges, deposits, maintenance advance | varies by society | |
| Interiors, moving | buyer's choice | The reel folds these into its 15% |
| TDS | buyer deducts 1% of the price when it is Rs 50 lakh or more and deposits it (old s.194-IA; renumbered in the Income-tax Act 2025) | Forgetting it brings interest and notices |

Example: a Rs 60 lakh resale flat in Gujarat: stamp duty about Rs 2.94 lakh + registration about Rs 60,000 + brokerage about Rs 70,800 (1% + GST) + legal and loan costs about Rs 50,000 = about Rs 4.75 lakh, or about 7.9% before interiors. The same price under construction would add Rs 3 lakh of GST.

## Caveats
- Stamp-duty sources conflict, especially for Maharashtra and Karnataka; check igrmaharashtra.gov.in, revenue.delhi.gov.in, kaveri.karnataka.gov.in, garvi.gujarat.gov.in on the day.
- CIBIL cut-offs and rates are lender policy and move with the repo rate.
- Not legal or financial advice.

## What the source got wrong
- "Add 15% to the price, that is the actual cost" mixes statutory costs (6-8% in most states) with discretionary interiors and moving; for an under-construction flat GST alone adds 5%, so 15% may be too low there and too high for a bare resale flat.
- "Below 750 CIBIL, the loan will be rejected": not a rule; many lenders lend from 650-700 at higher rates.
- Rs 18,000 lawyer saving Rs 70 lakh and Rs 13 lakh saved on the eighth viewing are unverified anecdotes.
- Omits TDS on purchases of Rs 50 lakh or more and the GST difference between ready and under-construction flats, both of which change the budget.
