# Tax red lines (India, from 1 Apr 2026)

## The point in one line
Fake documents, bogus expenses, structured cash and unexplained money are misreporting. Each costs a penalty of 200% of the tax on top of the tax itself, with possible prosecution. Every one of them has a legal alternative that, for most freelancers, saves as much.

## Red lines and consequences

| Practice | Section (old) | Consequence (as of Oct 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Fake rent receipts or HRA; fake 80C/80D/donation proofs; bogus political or charity donations | s.439 (270A) | Misreporting: penalty of **200% of the tax**. Ordinary under-reporting is 50% |
| Bogus expenses, inflated contractor bills, personal spending booked as business | s.439; s.444 for false entries in books | Disallowance plus penalty |
| Unexplained cash, deposits, investments, expenditure (including fake "agricultural income") | s.102-106 (68-69D); rate s.195 | Taxed at a flat **30%** (cut from 60% by the Finance Act 2026), plus misreporting penalty |
| Receiving Rs 2,00,000 or more in cash from one person in a day, for one transaction or for one event (including splitting to stay under) | s.186 (269ST); penalty s.451 (271DA) | Penalty of **100% of the amount received** |
| Cash loan or deposit of Rs 20,000 or more | s.185 (269SS); penalty s.450 (271D) | Penalty of 100% of the amount |
| Cash business expense over Rs 10,000 a day to one person | s.36(4) (40A(3)) | Expense disallowed |
| Paying family members who do no work | s.99 (64) | Income clubbed back, plus misreporting |
| Not filing or late filing | s.428(a), s.263(4) | Rs 1,000 / Rs 5,000 fee; losses not carried forward; belated return only up to 9 months after the tax year |
| Prosecution for wilful evasion | - | Simple imprisonment of up to **2 years** (down from 7), graded by the amount evaded (Budget 2026) |

**Cash and bank reporting (the department sees it in AIS)** [verify under Rules 2026]:
- Cash deposits of Rs 10 lakh or more a year in savings accounts (Rs 50 lakh in current accounts).
- Credit-card bills paid in cash of Rs 1 lakh or more.
- 2% TDS on cash withdrawals above Rs 1 crore.

**GST** [verify]:
- Not registering above Rs 20 lakh turnover: tax plus interest plus a penalty.
- Collecting GST without depositing it: prosecution above thresholds.
- Fake ITC invoices: the same.
- Returns cannot be filed more than 3 years after the due date (per secondary sources).

## The way back (legal)
- **Updated return (s.263(6)):** up to 48 months from the end of the following FY, with additional tax of 25% / 50% / 60% / 70% depending on how late it is. Budget 2026 also allows one after a reassessment notice, at a further 10%.
- **Penalty immunity:** pay 100% of the tax as additional tax (120% for unexplained cash credits), from Budget 2026.
- **Foreign assets:** FAST-DS 2026, a six-month one-time scheme for small taxpayers. Up to Rs 1 crore of undisclosed foreign income or assets: 30% tax plus 30% additional tax. Assets up to Rs 5 crore where tax was paid but the asset was not reported: Rs 1 lakh fee. Check whether the window is open.

## Example
A freelancer with Rs 20 lakh of receipts considers fake rent receipts worth Rs 2.4 lakh to claim a rent deduction in the old regime:
- Under presumptive, the new regime already gives **nil** tax (deemed income Rs 10 lakh, s.156 rebate). The fake receipts save nothing.
- Even in a case where they "saved" Rs 50,000, detection costs that Rs 50,000 plus a Rs 1,00,000 penalty (200%), plus interest.

## Legal alternatives to suggest
- Presumptive s.58, which needs no expense bills.
- The new regime.
- Bank and UPI collection.
- Genuine family payments on books.
- Employer NPS.
- LTCG harvesting.
- A company only when profit is retained.

## Caveats
- Penalty sections are from the Act text (a CA-hosted copy attributed to the Department). Cash-reporting thresholds are old-Rule values not re-checked.
- Not legal or tax advice. If something has already been misreported, see a CA before any notice arrives.
