# 29 named design styles, translated into our directions

## The point in one line
Clients and designers name styles ("make it bento", "something Y2K", "glassmorphism"); this table turns each name into one of the 16 directions in the web-design skill, or says why it should stay an accent rather than a whole site.

## The list (as shown in the reel, in order)
Minimalism, Maximalism, Swiss Design, Brutalism, Surrealism, Neo-Brutalism, Neo-classical, Neumorphism, Scrapbook, Glassmorphism, Claymorphism, Bento Grid, Pixel Art, Conceptual Sketch, Luxury Typography, Editorial Design, Y2K Aesthetic, Ethereal, Bohemian, Dark Mode UI, Cyberpunk, Anthropomorphic, Victorian, Cybercore, Synthwave, Graffiti, Gothic, Mixed Media, Wabi Sabi.

The reel's examples were print posters (magazine covers, event posters), not websites. Many of these are graphic-design or illustration styles; only some work as a whole web page.

## How to use it
- When a client names a style, translate it to a direction and confirm with one reference screenshot; the style word alone means different things to different people.
- For clinics and hospitals the realistic shortlist stays Soft and Calm, Quiet Editorial, Corporate Trust, Luxury Monochrome (premium) and Warm Organic (Ayurveda, wellness). Styles that cut contrast (neumorphism, heavy glass, ethereal pastel text) conflict with accessibility for older patients.
- Several names here are on our AI-tell list when used by reflex (bento everywhere, glass panels, purple-blue gradients, neon dark mode); see web/ai-slop-design-tells.md.

## Caveats
- Style names are informal and overlap (Brutalism vs Neo-Brutalism, Cyberpunk vs Cybercore vs Synthwave); definitions above are our working definitions, not a standard.
- The reel gave names only; fit-for-purpose judgements are ours.

## What the source got wrong
- Nothing factual; it is a name list. Whisper heard "Victorian" as "Victoria" (on-screen list confirms Victorian) and "Neomorphism" (on-screen: Neumorphism).
- Presenting all 29 as equally usable for websites is misleading: a third of them are illustration or poster styles that work only as an accent.
