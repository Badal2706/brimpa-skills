## 1. The new Act: what changed and when

- The Income-tax Act, 2025 (Act 30 of 2025) came into force on **1 April 2026** (s.1(3)). Section 536(1) repeals the 1961 Act.
- Under the savings clause in s.536(2), the 1961 Act still governs every tax year that began before 1 April 2026: assessments, penalties and appeals. **FY 2025-26 income is filed in 2026 under the 1961 Act.** Income earned from 1 April 2026 falls under the 2025 Act.
- "Tax year" (s.3) replaces "previous year" and "assessment year". For a new business, the first tax year runs from the date of set-up to 31 March.
- Budget 2026 (1 Feb 2026) made **no change to income-tax rates or slabs** for TY 2026-27.
- The Income-tax Rules, 2026 were notified on 20 Mar 2026 (G.S.R. 198(E)). Forms are renumbered. Per secondary sources: Form 16 becomes Form 130, Form 26AS becomes Form 168, and the audit forms 3CA/3CB/3CD merge into Form 26. The AY 2026-27 filing season still used the old ITR-1 to ITR-7.
- Per secondary sources, about 819 sections in 47 chapters became 536 sections in 23 chapters.
- The Act text used for this skill came from a Department-attributed booklet hosted on a CA firm's site, because incometaxindia.gov.in refused automated access. [verify] Re-check key sections against the official PDF.

## 2. New regime (default), s.202

| Total income (Rs) | Rate |
|---|---|
| 0 - 4,00,000 | Nil |
| 4,00,001 - 8,00,000 | 5% |
| 8,00,001 - 12,00,000 | 10% |
| 12,00,001 - 16,00,000 | 15% |
| 16,00,001 - 20,00,000 | 20% |
| 20,00,001 - 24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above 24,00,000 | 30% |

- **Rebate, s.156(2) (old 87A).** If total income is at most Rs 12,00,000, a resident individual gets a rebate of up to Rs 60,000, so tax is nil. The rebate does not apply to tax on s.198 LTCG (s.198(7)). Whether it applies to s.196 STCG is not confirmed in the text [verify].
- **Marginal relief, s.156(2)(b).** Just above Rs 12 lakh, the tax cannot exceed the income above Rs 12 lakh. Relief ends at about Rs 12,70,588 of non-salary income: at that point the slab tax (60,000 + 15% of the excess) equals the excess.
- **Standard deduction, s.19(1) Table Sl. 2.** Salary or pension only: Rs 75,000 in the new regime, Rs 50,000 in the old. Salary up to Rs 12,75,000 pays nil tax in the new regime.
- **What the new regime keeps (s.202(2)).**
  - Business expenses and depreciation.
  - Employer NPS contribution, s.124(1)-(2): up to 14% of salary.
  - Agniveer (s.125(2)) and s.146 (additional employee cost).
  - Interest on a let-out property against that property's rent.
- **What the new regime drops.** HRA, LTA, professional tax, self-occupied home-loan interest, setting house-property loss against other heads, and every other Chapter VIII deduction (80C, 80D, 80CCD(1B) and so on).
- **Surcharge.** 10% above Rs 50 lakh, 15% above Rs 1 crore, 25% above Rs 2 crore. The cap in the new regime is 25%. On dividends and capital gains (s.196/197/198) it is capped at 15%. Marginal relief applies at each threshold.
- **Cess.** 4% Health and Education Cess on tax plus surcharge, with no marginal relief.

| Total income | Slab tax | After rebate/relief | With 4% cess | Effective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rs 12,00,000 | 60,000 | 0 | **0** | 0% |
| Rs 12,50,000 | 67,500 | 50,000 (relief) | **52,000** | 4.2% |
| Rs 15,00,000 | 1,05,000 | 1,05,000 | **1,09,200** | 7.3% |
| Rs 20,00,000 | 2,00,000 | 2,00,000 | **2,08,000** | 10.4% |
| Rs 24,00,000 | 3,00,000 | 3,00,000 | **3,12,000** | 13.0% |
| Rs 30,00,000 | 4,80,000 | 4,80,000 | **4,99,200** | 16.6% |
| Rs 40,00,000 | 7,80,000 | 7,80,000 | **8,11,200** | 20.3% |
| Rs 50,00,000 | 10,80,000 | 10,80,000 | **11,23,200** | 22.5% |

Shortcut above Rs 24 lakh: slab tax = Rs 3,00,000 + 30% of (income - 24,00,000), then add 4% cess.

## 3. Old regime (opt-out)

| Income (Rs) | Below 60 | 60-79 (resident) | 80+ (resident) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to 2,50,000 | Nil | Nil | Nil |
| 2,50,001 - 3,00,000 | 5% | Nil | Nil |
| 3,00,001 - 5,00,000 | 5% | 5% | Nil |
| 5,00,001 - 10,00,000 | 20% | 20% | 20% |
| Above 10,00,000 | 30% | 30% | 30% |

- **Rebate, s.156(1).** Up to Rs 12,500 if total income is at most Rs 5 lakh.
- **Surcharge.** 10% above Rs 50 lakh, 15% above Rs 1 crore, 25% above Rs 2 crore, 37% above Rs 5 crore (capped at 15% on dividends and capital gains). Cess 4%.
- Shortcut above Rs 10 lakh: slab tax = Rs 1,12,500 + 30% of (income - 10,00,000), then add 4% cess.

| Extra own NPS | s.124(3) (80CCD(1B)) | Rs 50,000 |

| Savings interest / seniors' deposit interest | s.153 (80TTA/80TTB) | Rs 10,000 / Rs 50,000 |

**How to switch, s.202(4):**
- **With business or professional income (a freelancer):** opt for the old regime in the prescribed form by the s.263(1) due date (31 Aug 2027 for a non-audit case). The choice carries forward. You can withdraw it **only once**, and after that you can never opt back in while you have business income. Treat it as a near-permanent decision.
- **With no business income:** choose fresh each year in the return. Per secondary sources, a belated return cannot opt for the old regime.

## 4. Old vs new break-even

This is the total old-regime deduction needed just to *match* the new-regime tax, at the same gross income (cess included, verified by calculator, 30 Oct 2026).

| Gross income | Non-salary (freelancer) | Salaried (deductions beyond the standard deduction) |
|---|---|---|
| Up to Rs 12 lakh (Rs 12.75 lakh salary) | Never: new-regime tax is nil | Never |
| Rs 15 lakh | Rs 5.38 lakh | Rs 5.44 lakh |
| Rs 20 lakh | Rs 7.09 lakh | Rs 7.09 lakh |
| Rs 22.5 lakh | Rs 7.50 lakh | - |
| Rs 24 lakh and above | Rs 7.75 lakh (flat) | Rs 7.88 lakh at 24 lakh, Rs 8.0 lakh from about 30 lakh |

Above Rs 24 lakh the gap is fixed, because both regimes are at 30% at the margin.

A realistic old-regime stack for a freelancer with senior parents:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| s.123 (80C) | Rs 1,50,000 |
| s.124(3) (80CCD(1B)) | Rs 50,000 |
| s.126 self | Rs 25,000 |
| s.126 senior parents | Rs 50,000 |
| **Subtotal** | **Rs 2,75,000** |
| s.134 rent (if eligible) | up to Rs 60,000 |
| Self-occupied home-loan interest | up to Rs 2,00,000 |
| **Maximum** | **about Rs 5,35,000** |

Even the maximum is below every break-even above Rs 15 lakh. **For a freelancer the old regime wins only with unusually large deductions.** Always run both regimes anyway; never assume.

## 5. Presumptive taxation, s.58 (old 44ADA / 44AD)

### Profession: s.58 Table Sl. 3 (old 44ADA)

- **Who.** A "specified assessee": a resident individual or a partnership firm, **not an LLP** (s.58(11)(b)), carrying on a "specified profession" under s.62(4).
- **Is IT a specified profession?** s.62(4)(a) lists "legal, medical, engineering, architectural, accountancy, technical consultancy, interior decoration, **information technology** or company secretary". This is now in the Act itself; under the 1961 Act it was only notified.
- **Limit.** Gross receipts up to **Rs 50 lakh**, or up to **Rs 75 lakh** if cash receipts are at most 5% of gross receipts. A non-account-payee cheque or draft counts as cash (s.58(9)).
- **Deemed profit.** **50% of gross receipts**, or more if you declare more.
- **No further deductions (s.58(4)).** No expense, loss or allowance is allowed against the deemed income. Depreciation is treated as already allowed (s.58(6)). Old-regime Chapter VIII deductions still reduce total income.
- **Declaring less than 50%.** If total income is above the basic exemption, you must keep books (s.62) and get a tax audit (s.63) (s.58(3)).

### Business: s.58 Table Sl. 1 (old 44AD)

- **Who.** A resident individual, HUF or firm (not an LLP) that **does not carry on a specified profession** (s.58(11)(a)(iii)) and earns no commission, brokerage or agency income.
- **Limit.** Turnover up to Rs 2 crore, or Rs 3 crore if cash receipts are at most 5%.
- **Deemed profit.** 6% of receipts received by bank or online by the return due date; 8% on the rest.
- **Five-year lock (s.58(7)-(8)).** Opt in, then declare lower profit in any of the next five years, and you are barred for five years and must keep books and get an audit.

### The IT-services judgement call (aggressive position: CA only)

Building websites and software for clinics and hospitals under contract can be argued to be either:
- an IT **profession** (Sl. 3: 50% deemed, up to Rs 75 lakh), or
- a software-development **business** (Sl. 1: 6%/8% deemed, up to Rs 3 crore).

Because s.62(4) names "information technology" explicitly, **the conservative reading is Sl. 3 (50%)**. Claiming 6% on IT services is an aggressive position. Present it only as a question for a CA, with the tax difference quantified, and never as the default. Selling a packaged product or SaaS licence leans further towards "business" [verify]. A mix of custom builds, SaaS licences, AMC and hosting resale needs a CA view on how to split it [verify].

| Rs 20 lakh | Rs 10 lakh | Rs 0 | 0% |
| Rs 24 lakh | Rs 12 lakh | Rs 0 | 0% |
| Rs 30 lakh | Rs 15 lakh | Rs 1,09,200 | 3.6% |
| Rs 40 lakh | Rs 20 lakh | Rs 2,08,000 | 5.2% |
| Rs 45 lakh | Rs 22.5 lakh | Rs 2,73,000 | 6.1% |

This is the benchmark any company structure must beat. **Above Rs 50 lakh, the higher limit depends entirely on cash receipts staying at or below 5%.** Above Rs 75 lakh, or with too much cash, presumptive is lost: you then need books, a tax audit (profession receipts over Rs 50 lakh) and tax on actual profit.

Whether gross receipts for s.58 include the GST you collect depends on how the books record it; normally GST shown separately is excluded [verify].

## 6. Books of account and tax audit

- **Books, specified profession (s.62(1)(a)).** Required for anyone carrying on a specified profession; the Rules prescribe which books. The old Rule 6F applied only above Rs 1.5 lakh of receipts [verify for Rules 2026].
- **Books, business or other profession (s.62(2)(d)).** Required for an individual or HUF if income exceeds Rs 2.5 lakh or turnover exceeds Rs 25 lakh in any of the three preceding years, or if profit is claimed below the presumptive rate.
- **Tax audit (s.63 Table):**
  - Business: turnover over Rs 1 crore, or over Rs 10 crore if cash receipts and cash payments are each at most 5%.
  - **Profession: gross receipts over Rs 50 lakh.**
  - Anyone declaring below the presumptive profit (subject to s.58(3)).
- **Presumptive filers are exempt (s.63(2)).** An IT professional with Rs 50-75 lakh receipts and cash at or below 5% avoids audit by declaring 50% under s.58.
- **Audit report deadline (s.63(5)(a)).** One month before the return due date.
- **Fee for failing to get audited (s.428(c), substituted by the Finance Act 2026).** Rs 75,000 for a delay up to one month, Rs 1,50,000 beyond. This replaces the old 0.5%-of-turnover penalty.

## 8. Advance tax and interest

- **Who pays (s.404).** Anyone whose tax for the year, after TDS, is Rs 10,000 or more.
- **Normal instalments (s.408(1)), cumulative:** 15% by 15 June, 45% by 15 September, 75% by 15 December, 100% by 15 March.
- **Presumptive under s.58 Sl. 1 or Sl. 3 (s.408(2)):** the **whole amount by 15 March**. Anything paid by 31 March counts as advance tax (s.408(3)).
- **s.424 (old 234B).** 1% a month from 1 April after the tax year if advance tax paid is below 90% of the assessed tax.
- **s.425 (old 234C).** 3% flat for each June, September and December shortfall (none if at least 12% was paid by June and 36% by September), plus 1% for a March shortfall. Presumptive filers pay 1% on the 15 March shortfall only. No interest on shortfall from capital gains, dividends or first-time business income if it is paid in a later instalment.
- **Late return.** Interest under s.423 (old 234A) at 1% a month [verify exact text]. Late-filing fee s.428(a): Rs 1,000 if income is up to Rs 5 lakh, Rs 5,000 otherwise.
- **Senior citizen with no business income:** no advance tax under the old law (s.207(2)) [verify for the new Act].

## 10. Claiming actual expenses (books instead of presumptive)

- **Test (s.34):** spent "wholly and exclusively" for the business, with invoices.
- **Depreciation (s.33):** at block rates in the Rules. Computers and software were 40% WDV under the old Rules (half if used under 180 days in the year); per secondary sources Rules 2026 kept the rates [verify].
- **Usual costs:** internet, phone, hosting, domains, SaaS and AI tools, contractors, client travel, courses, a documented share of home-office rent and power [verify home office].
- **Disallowed:**
  - Personal or capital spending.
  - Cash payments over **Rs 10,000 a day to one person** (s.36(4), old 40A(3)).
  - Payments where TDS was due but not deducted (30% disallowance under the old 40(a)(ia)) [verify the new section].
- **When books beat presumptive.** Only if real costs (a team, contractors) exceed about 50% of receipts, because books also bring an audit above Rs 50 lakh receipts.

## 11. Capital gains and harvesting

**Holding periods (s.2(101)):** short-term means 12 months or less for listed securities and equity-oriented fund units, and 24 months or less for everything else (property, gold, unlisted shares).

| Asset | STCG | LTCG |
|---|---|---|
| Listed equity / equity MF / business trust units (STT paid) | **20%** (s.196) | **12.5% on gains above Rs 1,25,000 a year** (s.198) |
| Debt MF (more than 65% debt) bought on or after 1 Apr 2023; MLDs; unlisted bonds transferred from 23 Jul 2024 | Always short-term at slab (s.76) | - |
| Other assets (property, physical gold, gold ETF/FoF, international funds, unlisted shares) | Slab | **12.5% without indexation** (s.197) |
| Land or building bought before 23 Jul 2024 (resident individual/HUF) | - | Lower of 12.5% without indexation or 20% with indexation (s.197(3)) |

- **Unused basic exemption** can absorb STCG/LTCG for a resident individual (s.196(2), 197(2), 198(3)). Chapter VIII deductions cannot.
- **Buybacks (Budget 2026):** from TY 2026-27, buyback proceeds are taxed as capital gains again, not as dividend.
- **F&O STT raised (Budget 2026):** futures 0.02% to 0.05%; options premium 0.1% to 0.15%; options exercise 0.125% to 0.15%.
- **Interest expense** is no longer deductible against dividend or MF income (Budget 2026).
- **Reinvestment exemptions:**
  - s.82 (old 54): LTCG on a house, reinvested in one house in India within 1 year before or 2 years after the sale (3 years to build). Capped where cost or gain exceeds Rs 10 crore.
  - s.85 (old 54EC): LTCG on land or building into specified bonds within 6 months, up to Rs 50 lakh.
  - s.86 (old 54F): LTCG on any other asset into a house, proportionate [verify detailed conditions].
- **Gain harvesting:** sell and rebuy equity or equity-MF units each FY to book up to Rs 1,25,000 of LTCG tax-free. This saves up to Rs 15,625 of tax (12.5%), or Rs 16,250 with cess, every year, and raises your cost base. India has no wash-sale rule [verify]. Watch exit loads.
- **Loss harvesting:** short-term losses offset STCG and LTCG; long-term losses offset only LTCG [verify]. Losses carry forward 8 years **only if the return is filed by the due date** (s.263(1)(a)(viii)).
- **Gold ETFs and FoFs** are generally not "specified mutual funds" [verify]: ETFs become long-term after 12 months, FoFs after 24, both at 12.5%.

## 12. Tax-advantaged products

- **PPF:** 7.1% for Oct-Dec 2026 (per secondary sources: Q3 FY27 small-savings notice of 30 Sep 2026). Interest is tax-free in both regimes; the s.123 deduction is old regime only. 15-year tenure, Rs 500 to Rs 1.5 lakh per FY; deposit by the 5th to earn that month's interest [verify].
- **Other small savings, Q3 FY27 (per secondary sources):** SCSS 8.2%, Sukanya Samriddhi 8.2%, NSC 7.7%, KVP 7.5%, PO savings 4.0%.
- **EPF/VPF:** 8.25% for FY 2025-26 (per secondary sources: CBT decision 2 Mar 2026, ratified June 2026). Interest is taxable on your own contributions above Rs 2.5 lakh a year (Rs 5 lakh with no employer contribution), for contributions from 1 Apr 2021 (Schedule II). Relevant only if Badal draws a salary.
- **NPS:**
  - Employer contribution up to 14% of salary is deductible even in the new regime (s.124(1)-(2)).
  - Own contribution counts within Rs 1.5 lakh, plus Rs 50,000 under s.124(3): old regime only.
  - A self-employed person gets **no deduction in the new regime** for own NPS.
  - Exit: 60% of the corpus is tax-free (Schedule II Sl. 6); annuity income is taxable.
  - Per secondary sources, the PFRDA amendment of Dec 2025 lets non-government subscribers take up to 80% as a lump sum. The Act still exempts only 60%, so tax on the extra 20% is unclear [verify].
  - Employer PF + NPS + superannuation above Rs 7.5 lakh a year is a taxable perquisite (s.17(1)(h)).
- **ELSS:** counts within s.123 (old regime only); 3-year lock-in [verify]. In the new regime it is just an equity fund with a lock-in, so a plain index fund is usually better.
- **Sovereign Gold Bonds:** from Budget 2026, tax-free maturity applies only to original subscribers who hold until maturity, so bonds bought on the exchange lose it. The 2.5% interest is taxed at slab. No new tranche has been seen since Feb 2024 [verify].
- **FD interest:** taxed at slab. TDS above Rs 50,000 a year (Rs 1 lakh for seniors). Budget 2026 lets you file 15G/15H through depositories. DICGC insures Rs 5 lakh per depositor per bank [verify].
- **Senior parents (old regime):** basic exemption Rs 3 lakh (60-79) or Rs 5 lakh (80+); s.153 Rs 50,000 on deposit interest; s.126 Rs 50,000. The new regime has no age-based slabs.

## 13. Insurance and its tax treatment

- **GST:** individual term-life and individual health premiums (including floaters) are GST-exempt from 22 Sep 2025. Group policies still carry 18% (per secondary sources: DFS FAQ, Oct 2025).
- **Term life:** the s.123 premium deduction is old regime only. Proceeds are exempt under Schedule II subject to premium-to-sum-assured conditions. ULIPs with annual premium above Rs 2.5 lakh, and other policies above Rs 5 lakh issued from 1 Apr 2023, lose the maturity exemption.
- **Health:** s.126 limits above (old regime only). A multi-year premium paid upfront is spread across the years. No deduction in the new regime.
- **Sizing** is in `money-system.md`.

## 14. Firm, LLP, company; salary vs dividend; DPIIT

| Structure | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietor | Individual slabs; presumptive available (s.58) | Business income is the owner's income. Drawings are not deductible. |
| Partnership firm (non-LLP) | 30% + 12% surcharge above Rs 1 crore + 4% cess | Presumptive available. Partner remuneration deductible within s.35(e): on the first Rs 6 lakh of book profit, the higher of Rs 3 lakh or 90%; then 60% of the balance. Partner interest capped at 12% p.a. |
| LLP | 30% + cess (surcharge as for a firm) | **No presumptive** (s.58(11)). AMT at 18.5% under s.206 only if certain deductions are claimed. |
| Pvt Ltd, s.200 (old 115BAA) | 22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess = **25.168%** | No MAT for s.200 companies, as under the 1961 Act [verify for s.206]. Chapter VIII deductions are barred except s.146/148, so **no s.140 startup holiday** under s.200. |
| Pvt Ltd, s.199 (default) | 25% if TY 2024-25 turnover was at most Rs 400 crore, else 30%; surcharge 7% above Rs 1 crore, 12% above Rs 10 crore; cess 4% | |
| MAT (old-regime companies) | **14%**, a final tax with no new credit from 1 Apr 2026 (cut from 15%) | Credit up to 31 Mar 2026 can be used only by companies moving to the new regime, up to 25% of tax. |

**Salary vs dividend:**
- **Dividend:** taxed at slab in the shareholder's hands (no DDT since 2020) [verify], with 10% TDS. Surcharge on dividends is capped at 15%. It is paid out of profit that has already borne 25.168% company tax.
- **Director salary:** deductible for the company. With the Rs 75,000 standard deduction, **salary up to Rs 12,75,000 is tax-free** in the new regime. Salary must be reasonable for the work done [verify].
- **Employer NPS:** up to 14% of basic + DA is deductible for the company and not taxed on the employee, even in the new regime (subject to the Rs 7.5 lakh combined cap).

**Comparison** (verified; new regime; Rs 12.75 lakh salary, the rest company profit at 25.168%; ignores compliance cost and presumptive):

| Real profit | Proprietor tax | Company, rest retained | Company, rest paid as dividend |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rs 30 lakh | 16.6% | 14.5% | 25.8% |
| Rs 40 lakh | 20.3% | 17.1% | 31.5% |
| Rs 60 lakh | 26.3% | 19.8% | 37.2% |
| Rs 1 crore | 29.5% | 22.0% | 43.7% |

- A company wins only on profit that is **retained and reinvested**. Distributing it all is worse than a proprietorship.
- Against presumptive at Rs 50-75 lakh receipts (6.9-9.8% of receipts), a company loses clearly.
- Full worked arithmetic for Rs 40 lakh is in `worked-examples.md`.

**Compliance cost** [verify all]:
- **Pvt Ltd:** statutory audit every year, ROC filings (AOC-4 within 30 days of the AGM, MGT-7/7A within 60 days, ADT-1, DIR-3 KYC), board minutes, salary TDS returns. Roughly Rs 40,000 to Rs 1.5 lakh a year for a small company.
- **Small company** limits were raised to paid-up capital up to Rs 10 crore and turnover up to Rs 100 crore (per secondary sources: G.S.R. 880(E), 1 Dec 2025).
- **LLP:** Form 11 by 30 May, Form 8 by 30 Oct; audit only above Rs 40 lakh turnover or Rs 25 lakh contribution.

**DPIIT startup, s.140 (old 80-IAC).** 100% of profits are deductible for any 3 consecutive tax years out of the first 10, for a **company or LLP** incorporated from 1 Apr 2016 to before 1 Apr 2030, with turnover up to Rs 300 crore (raised from Rs 100 crore by the Finance Act 2026) and an Inter-Ministerial Board certificate. Not available to proprietorships or to s.200 companies. Angel tax was abolished from FY 2024-25 [verify].

**When a company makes sense:**
1. Receipts above Rs 75 lakh, or cash above 5%, so presumptive is gone and books plus audit are needed anyway.
2. Real profit consistently above about Rs 40-50 lakh, much of it retained for hiring or a product.
3. Liability ring-fencing for hospital contracts handling patient data (DPDP exposure, indemnities). This often matters more than tax.
4. Clients or tenders require a company; equity investors, ESOPs or DPIIT status are wanted.

**Not worth it:** a solo developer at Rs 20-75 lakh receipts who spends most of the profit. Stay a proprietor on s.58 Sl. 3.

## 15. Penalties and enforcement

| What | Section (old) | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Under-reporting income | s.439 (270A) | 50% of the tax |
| Misreporting (fake rent receipts, fake 80C/80D/donation proofs, bogus expenses, suppressed receipts) | s.439 (270A) | **200% of the tax**. Immunity only by paying 100% of the tax as additional tax (120% for unexplained cash credits), from Budget 2026 |
| Unexplained cash, credits, investments, expenditure | s.102-106 (68-69D) | Taxed at **30%** under s.195 (cut from 60% by the Finance Act 2026), plus the misreporting penalty |
| False entries in books | s.444 | Penalty |
| Cash receipt of Rs 2,00,000 or more from one person in a day, one transaction or one event | s.186 (269ST); penalty s.451 (271DA) | **100% of the amount received** |
| Cash loan or deposit of Rs 20,000 or more | s.185 (269SS); penalty s.450 (271D) | 100% of the amount |
| Cash expense over Rs 10,000 a day to one person | s.36(4) (40A(3)) | Disallowed (Rs 35,000 for goods-carriage hire) |
| Prosecution for evasion | - | Simple imprisonment of up to **2 years** (down from 7), graded by the amount evaded (Budget 2026) |
| Late return | s.428(a) (234F) | Rs 1,000 / Rs 5,000 fee; losses cannot be carried forward |
| No tax audit when required | s.428(c) | Rs 75,000 / Rs 1,50,000 fee |

- **Belated return:** up to 9 months after the tax year (s.263(4)).
- **Updated return (s.263(6)):** up to 48 months from the end of the following FY, with additional tax of 25% / 50% / 60% / 70% depending on the year. Budget 2026 also allows it after a reassessment notice at a further 10%.
- **Cash reporting** (old Rule 114E values; [verify] under Rules 2026): banks report cash deposits of Rs 10 lakh or more a year in savings accounts (Rs 50 lakh in current accounts); credit-card bills paid in cash of Rs 1 lakh or more; 2% TDS on cash withdrawals above Rs 1 crore. All of it shows in your AIS.
- **Foreign assets:** Budget 2026 created a one-time six-month disclosure scheme for small taxpayers (FAST-DS 2026). Undisclosed foreign income or assets up to Rs 1 crore: 30% tax plus 30% additional tax. Assets up to Rs 5 crore where tax was paid but the asset was not declared: Rs 1 lakh fee. Check whether the window is still open [verify].
