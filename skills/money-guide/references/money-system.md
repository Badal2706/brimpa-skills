# Monthly money system for an irregular-income freelancer

Status date: 30 Oct 2026. Most of this file is **practical rules of thumb, not law**. The legal limits it relies on (cash rules, the 5% cash condition, advance-tax dates, rates) are cited with sections. Present the rules of thumb as such ("a common rule is..."), and adapt them to the user's numbers.

## 1. Three accounts

| Account | Receives | Pays |
|---|---|---|
| **Business current account** | All client money, nothing else | Business costs (hosting, SaaS, contractors, laptop); the monthly salary transfer; tax-vault transfers |
| **Tax vault** (a separate savings account or sweep FD) | GST collected plus the income-tax bucket | GST returns, advance tax, self-assessment tax |
| **Personal account** | One fixed "salary" transfer a month | Life: rent, EMIs, SIPs, spending |

Why this works: presumptive receipts, GST turnover and the 5% cash test can then be read straight off one bank statement, and personal spending never eats tax money.

## 2. Pay yourself a salary

- **Salary** = 70-80% of your trailing 12-month average **net income** (receipts minus business costs minus tax).
- Leave the rest in the business account as a buffer.
- Take a quarterly "bonus" only when the business buffer is above target (section 4).
- Review the figure every quarter. Lower it at once after two weak months; raise it only after two strong quarters.

**Example:**
- Average receipts: Rs 3,75,000 a month (Rs 45 lakh a year).
- Business costs: Rs 25,000.
- Income tax at about 6.1%: Rs 22,750.
- Net = 3,75,000 - 25,000 - 22,750 = Rs 3,27,250.
- Salary at 75% = **about Rs 2,45,000 a month**. The remaining ~Rs 82,000 a month builds the buffer.

## 3. Tax buckets: what % to set aside

On **every** receipt, the same day:

1. **GST: 100% of the GST collected** goes to the vault. It is the government's money.
2. **Income tax:** set aside your effective rate on fee receipts (excluding GST), plus about 1 point of margin. Then subtract any TDS the client already deducted.

**Effective rate on receipts (s.58 Sl. 3 presumptive, new regime, no other income; verified 30 Oct 2026):**

| Annual receipts | Tax as % of receipts | Suggested bucket |
|---|---|---|
| Up to Rs 24 lakh | 0% | 0-1% |
| Rs 30 lakh | 3.6% | 5% |
| Rs 40 lakh | 5.2% | 6% |
| Rs 50 lakh | 6.9% | 8% |
| Rs 60 lakh | 8.3% | 9% |
| Rs 75 lakh | 9.8% | 11% |
| Books and actual profit, high-profit year | 20-30% of profit | 30% of profit |

If you have a salary as well, use the marginal rate on the freelance part instead (example 4 in `worked-examples.md`).

**Invoice examples** (a Rs 1,00,000 fee with 18% GST; Rs 45 lakh a year, so a bucket of about 7%):

| Client | Client pays | To GST vault | To income-tax vault | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Company deducting 10% TDS | 1,18,000 - 10,000 = 1,08,000 | 18,000 | 0 | TDS of 10,000 already exceeds the 7,000 bucket; track the 3,000 as a likely refund |
| Clinic, no TDS | 1,18,000 | 18,000 | 7,000 | |
| Foreign client under LUT | 1,00,000 (no GST) | 0 | 7,000 | Keep the FIRC/e-FIRA record |

- **Paying out of the vault:** pay advance tax (presumptive: the whole amount by 15 March; s.408(2)) and GST from it.
- **Reconcile every quarter:** compare the TDS in AIS with your invoices.
- **Leftover:** whatever remains after the return is filed and the refund arrives goes to the emergency fund first.

## 4. Emergency fund

- **Personal fund:** 9-12 months of *essential* spending for an irregular freelancer, against the usual 6 months for a salaried person. This is a rule of thumb.
- **Business buffer:** 2-3 months of business costs, kept in the business account.
- **Where to keep it:** a sweep FD or a liquid fund. Debt-fund gains are taxed at slab (s.76), the same as FD interest, so choose on access and safety. DICGC insures Rs 5 lakh per depositor per bank [verify].
- **Order:** get health cover in place first, then fill the fund, then invest aggressively.

**Example:** essentials of Rs 60,000 a month x 10 months = Rs 6,00,000, plus a business buffer of 3 x Rs 25,000 = Rs 75,000.

## 5. Budgeting the salary

- Apply **50/30/20 to the salary you pay yourself**, not to gross receipts: 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% saving and investing.
- While the emergency fund is incomplete, use **50/20/30** (30% saving).
- Automate it: the salary lands, then the SIPs and the RD/PPF transfer go out on the next day, and what remains is spendable.
- **Surplus from a good month:** emergency fund first, then the business buffer, then investments. Never lifestyle.
- **Track spending in 5 buckets only:** home, food, transport, family, fun. More categories rarely survive three months.

## 6. Debt order

1. **Credit card:** pay the full statement balance every month. Revolving rates run around 36-45% a year [verify].
2. **Personal loans and BNPL:** clear them next.
3. **Car or home loan:** prepay only after the emergency fund and term/health cover are in place. In the new regime, self-occupied home-loan interest gives **no tax benefit** (s.202(2)), so prepaying it is a plain return equal to the loan rate.

**Deciding on a loan or card:**
- **Balloon or "low EMI" loans** (a large final payment): compare the total interest paid over the term, not the EMI. A smaller EMI with a balloon usually costs more in total.
- **Prepay or invest:** the hurdle is the loan's after-tax rate (the rate minus any tax benefit on its interest; in the new regime most personal, car and self-occupied home loans give none, so the full rate). An investment has to beat that after its own tax and risk, or prepay.
- **Is a reward card worth it:** yearly value = points earned x a realistic redemption value per point - annual fee including 18% GST - forex markup on foreign spends - convenience and processing fees. If that is under zero, use a no-fee card.
- **Paying income tax or GST by card:** only when the points earned beat the convenience fee on that payment; otherwise pay by net banking.
- **Car rule (house rule of thumb, the stricter of the common versions combined):** at least 20-30% down, a term of at most 4 years, and an EMI of at most 10% of the founder's own monthly salary (the fixed transfer in section 2, not business receipts). Fail any one: buy a cheaper car or wait.

**Rates as of 30 Oct 2026 (RBI site):** repo **5.50%** after a 25 bp increase at the October 2026 MPC; SDF 5.25%; MSF and Bank Rate 5.75%; CRR 3%; SLR 18%. EMIs on repo-linked loans will rise.

## 7. Credit score

All points here are [verify] practice.
- Pay every bill on time. Payment history weighs most.
- Keep card utilisation below 30% of the limit.
- Keep your oldest card open.
- Avoid clusters of loan enquiries.
- Pull the free annual report from each bureau (CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, CRIF). Bureaus update fortnightly from 1 Jan 2025 under RBI's 2024 directions.
- File ITRs on time. Lenders use two or three years of ITRs as a freelancer's income proof.

## 8. Insurance sizing

Rules of thumb; buy before you need them.
- **Health first.** A freelancer has no employer group cover.
  - A Rs 10-25 lakh family floater for a metro family, plus a super top-up.
  - A separate policy for parents.
  - Individual health and term premiums are GST-exempt from 22 Sep 2025, which makes them about 15% cheaper (per secondary sources).
  - Waiting periods for pre-existing disease were capped at 3 years by IRDAI's 2024 master circular [verify].
- **Term life**, if anyone depends on you:
  - Cover of 10-15x annual expenses or income, plus all loans, minus liquid investments.
  - Cover until the dependants are independent.
  - Buy pure term, not endowment or ULIP.
- **Business:** professional indemnity and cyber-liability cover for hospital and clinic software contracts that touch patient data (DPDP exposure), plus cheap personal-accident and disability cover.

## 9. Invoicing and collections

- **Paperwork before work:** a contract or SOW with milestones, a change-request rate, IP transfer on payment, and a late-payment clause.
- **Advance:** take 30-50% upfront from new clients.
- **Invoice the day a milestone is accepted.** GST allows up to 30 days [verify], but waiting only delays your cash.
- **Show GST as a separate line,** and state the SAC (see `gst-for-it-services.md`).
- **Terms:** Net 7 or Net 15, with bank and UPI details printed on the invoice.
- **Bank or UPI only:**
  - Receiving Rs 2,00,000 or more in cash from one person in a day, for one transaction or for one event is barred (s.186). The penalty is 100% of the amount (s.451).
  - Cash above 5% of receipts also loses the Rs 75 lakh presumptive limit (s.58) and the higher audit limit.
- **Follow-up cadence:**
  - Due date: a reminder.
  - +7 days: a call.
  - +15 days: pause work.
  - +30 days: a formal notice.
- **Udyam (MSME) registration:** gives micro and small enterprises the 45-day payment rule and compound interest on late payments from buyers [verify].
- **Every quarter:** reconcile client TDS with AIS. Chase any client who deducted TDS but did not deposit it, because you cannot claim it until it shows.

## 10. Retirement and SIPs

- **SIPs:** automate a broad index-fund SIP the day after the salary transfer. No fund names in this skill; the user picks the fund.
- **Safe long-term money:** PPF at 7.1% for Oct-Dec 2026 (per secondary sources), tax-free.
- **NPS:** optional, for discipline.
  - Self-employed contributions get no deduction in the new regime.
  - Up to 80% can be taken as a lump sum at exit (per secondary sources), but only 60% is tax-exempt in the Act.
- **Harvesting:** book up to Rs 1,25,000 of equity LTCG every year (s.198). It is tax-free, and the higher cost base is worth up to Rs 15,625 a year (12.5%).
- **Increases:** raise the SIP with every salary increase, not with every good month.
- **Prompt for an allocation:** suggest the user decide an equity/debt split by age and goal, and write it down. Do not pick funds or time the market.

## 11. Scam red lines

All are [verify] practice unless cited.
- **"Digital arrest" video calls.** Police, CBI and customs never do this.
- **Fake trading or IPO apps,** and WhatsApp or Telegram "tip" groups promising returns.
- **"Task" jobs** that ask for a deposit.
- **UPI collect requests.** You never enter a PIN to *receive* money.
- **KYC or courier SMS links,** and requests to install screen-sharing apps.
- **Fake income-tax or GST notices.** Check every notice on the e-filing or GST portal.
- **"Overpaying" new clients** who then ask for a refund of the difference.
- **Before paying a broker or fund house,** check that SEBI's "@valid" UPI handle and SEBI Check confirm them (per secondary sources: live from 1 Oct 2025).
- **Report fraud immediately** on **1930** or cybercrime.gov.in. Speed decides whether money can be frozen.

## 12. The monthly checklist

| When | Do |
|---|---|
| Every receipt | Split into GST vault + income-tax bucket + business account |
| 1st | Salary transfer to the personal account; SIPs and PPF/RD go out on the 2nd |
| By the 10th to 13th | GST invoices uploaded (GSTR-1 or IFF) |
| By the 20th to 25th | GST paid (GSTR-3B or PMT-06) from the vault |
| Quarter end | AIS/TDS reconciliation; review the salary figure; check the 5% cash share and receipts against Rs 50 / 75 lakh |
| 15 Jun / Sep / Dec / Mar | Advance-tax instalments (presumptive: one payment by 15 March) |
| February | Year-end planning: regime check, LTCG harvest, old-regime investments if in the old regime, LUT for next year |
| Within a week of the year end | Send the books (or receipts list) to the CA |
